💡 How Evaluators & Judges Read this Graph:

The cyan curve is the path of the visual target. The blue (Left eye) and amber (Right eye) curves map real-time iris movement. When both eye curves closely mirror each other and track the target with smooth arcs, eye movement is coordinated. If one curve diverges, lags, or stops short, it indicates potential eye turn (squint) or muscle imbalance.

When Should a Pediatric Eye Specialist Be Consulted?

Clinical triage protocol for school screenings and community health workers

⚠️ Telemetry Warning Triggers:
  • Bilateral Movement Correlation persistently below 0.75
  • Symmetry Discrepancy greater than 10.0% across repeated tests
  • Fixation Stability RMS jitter exceeding 0.080
🚨 Physical Red Flags Observed by Parents:
  • Visible eye turn or squint (one eye turns in or out, even occasionally)
  • Child tilts or turns head sideways when looking at TV, books, or objects
  • Frequent eye rubbing, squinting, or complaining of headaches
  • Abnormal white or mismatched reflection in pupil during flash photos

Important Notice: NetraScreen is an early research triage tool designed to flag potential tracking anomalies for clinical review. It does not replace a comprehensive pediatric eye examination by a certified ophthalmologist or optometrist.
