💡 How Evaluators & Judges Read this Graph:

The cyan curve is the path of the visual target. The blue (Left eye) and amber (Right eye) curves map real-time iris movement. When both eye curves closely mirror each other and track the target with smooth arcs, eye movement is coordinated. If one curve diverges, lags, or stops short, it indicates potential eye turn (squint) or muscle imbalance.

Measures whether both eyes move together in unison in the same direction at the same speed. High correlation confirms synchronized binocular pursuit.

Measures involuntary eye shaking when holding gaze at a fixed point. A lower value indicates a calm, steady gaze without nystagmus or wandering attention.

Compares the maximum turning distance reached by the left vs right eye. A low difference indicates balanced extraocular muscle strength on both sides.

When Should an Eye Care Specialist (Ophthalmologist / Optometrist) Be Consulted?

Clinical triage protocol for schools, parents, and community health workers

⚠️ Telemetry Warning Triggers:
  • Bilateral Movement Correlation persistently below 0.75
  • Symmetry Discrepancy greater than 10.0% across repeated tests
  • Visual Acuity worse than 6/12 in either eye
  • Persistent low blink rate (<10/min) or OSDI dry eye score > 22
🚨 Physical Red Flags Observed by Parents:
  • Visible eye turn or squint (one eye turns in or out, even occasionally)
  • Child tilts or turns head sideways when looking at TV, books, or objects
  • Frequent eye rubbing, squinting, or complaining of headaches
  • Abnormal white or mismatched reflection in pupil during flash photos

Important Notice: NetraScreen is an early research triage and ergonomics tool designed to flag potential tracking anomalies and refractive concerns for clinical review. It does not replace a comprehensive pediatric eye examination by a certified ophthalmologist or optometrist.
