THE OPPORTUNITY

Accessible research. A considered path to care.

Our starting point is a practical question: can everyday phones make guided eye-movement observation easier to study outside specialist settings?

A working observation workflow

Consent, camera setup, four guided paths and a local report. Available as a web prototype and a native Android research app.

Privacy built into the experience

Camera frames are processed on the device. Reports are shared only through an explicit download or print action.

A defined next milestone

Representative-device testing, clinician review and supervised usability research. Clinical accuracy and pediatric validity remain to be established.

💡 Reading the observation plots

Target and iris paths use separate coordinate systems. Eye paths describe relative landmark movement. Differences may reflect tracking noise, head movement or task behavior; these plots cannot diagnose an eye condition.

Engineering & Pursuit Metrics

Calculated from accepted offline facial landmark telemetry
Bilateral Movement Correlation Review (< 0.75)
Unavailable

Correlation describes how the two landmark movement signals vary together. It does not establish normal binocular vision or clinical coordination.

Fixation Stability (RMS Jitter) Normal (< 0.050)
Unavailable

Within-hold variation of the iris landmarks. This is an experimental proxy affected by tracking noise and head movement.

Symmetry Discrepancy Discrepancy (> 10%)
Unavailable

Relative path-length difference between the paired iris landmarks. It is not a measurement of eye-muscle strength.

Accepted Frames Fraction High Quality (> 80%)
Unavailable

The fraction of frames accepted by the software capture checks. Acceptance does not establish clinical reliability.

Automated screening aid. Cannot replace clinical dilated examination or rule out pathology.
Research interpretation
  • Bilateral Movement Correlation persistently below 0.75
  • Symmetry Discrepancy greater than 10.0% across repeated tests
  • Fixation Stability RMS jitter exceeding 0.080
🚨 Physical Red Flags Observed by Parents:
  • Visible eye turn or squint (one eye turns in or out, even occasionally)
  • Child tilts or turns head sideways when looking at TV, books, or objects
  • Frequent eye rubbing, squinting, or complaining of headaches
  • Abnormal white or mismatched reflection in pupil during flash photos

Important Notice: NetraScreen is an early research triage tool designed to flag potential tracking anomalies for clinical review. It does not replace a comprehensive pediatric eye examination by a certified ophthalmologist or optometrist.
