WHY NETRA-SCREEN EXISTS

Earlier attention to children’s eyes. A more accessible starting point.

We are developing NETRA-SCREEN as a possible first step towards earlier professional assessment of eye-coordination concerns. The project asks whether a familiar smartphone and child-friendly tasks can support that goal.

Open research workspace →

The purpose: bring concerns to attention earlier

The project is motivated by children’s eye-coordination concerns, including squint. Our aim is a more accessible preliminary observation step that could support professional assessment after clinical validation.

What the prototype does today

Children follow a butterfly, fish or dot through four paths. The camera tracks eye landmarks on the device. NETRA produces movement plots and an observation report; camera images are not stored or uploaded.

What the report means

A record for supervised research review. It does not diagnose squint, lazy eye or another condition, confirm normal vision, or decide whether a child needs referral. Clinical screening capability still needs evaluation.

💡 Reading the observation plots

Target and iris paths use separate coordinate systems. Eye paths describe relative landmark movement. Differences may reflect tracking noise, head movement or task behavior; these plots cannot diagnose an eye condition.

Engineering & Pursuit Metrics

Calculated from accepted offline facial landmark telemetry

Unavailable

Correlation describes how the two landmark movement signals vary together. It does not establish normal binocular vision or clinical coordination.

When Should a Pediatric Eye Specialist Be Consulted?

General concern guidance · measurement thresholds are not clinically validated

Routine Checks Recommended
No Gross Asymmetry Flagged

Tracking symmetry and fixation stability remained within research screening limits. This does not exclude refractive errors; maintain regular pediatric vision checkups.

Automated screening aid. Cannot replace clinical dilated examination or rule out pathology.
Research interpretation
  • No validated clinical thresholds are available.
  • Repeat incomplete capture in a comfortable setup.
  • Discuss any eye-health concern with a qualified professional.
🚨 Physical Red Flags Observed by Parents:
  • Visible eye turn or squint (one eye turns in or out, even occasionally)
  • Child tilts or turns head sideways when looking at TV, books, or objects
  • Frequent eye rubbing, squinting, or complaining of headaches
  • Abnormal white or mismatched reflection in pupil during flash photos

Important Notice: NetraScreen is an early research triage tool designed to flag potential tracking anomalies for clinical review. It does not replace a comprehensive pediatric eye examination by a certified ophthalmologist or optometrist.
