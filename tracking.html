💡 Reading the research plots

Target and eye panels use different coordinate systems. Both eye paths share one scale. Path differences can reflect tracking noise, head movement or capture quality; they do not diagnose squint or muscle imbalance.

Engineering & Pursuit Metrics

Calculated from accepted offline facial landmark telemetry
Bilateral Movement Correlation Research only
Unavailable

Correlation of consecutive bilateral landmark displacement, including horizontal and vertical components. Constant or insufficient movement remains unavailable.

Fixation Stability (RMS Jitter) Research only
Unavailable

RMS variation around each held jump target after a settling period, in eye-width units. A research proxy affected by landmark noise and head movement.

Symmetry Discrepancy Research only
Unavailable

Relative difference in accumulated bilateral landmark path length. Not a measurement of muscle strength or ocular deviation.

Accepted Frames Fraction Research only
Unavailable

Accepted observations divided by actual processed camera frames. Capture heuristics do not establish medical reliability.

Routine Checks Recommended

Automated screening aid. Cannot replace clinical dilated examination or rule out pathology.
Understanding these measurements
  • No clinically validated reference ranges are available.
  • No automatic referral decision is made from telemetry.
  • Arrange an eye examination for vision concerns, regardless of these measurements.
Changes worth discussing with an eye professional
  • Visible eye turn or squint (one eye turns in or out, even occasionally)
  • Child tilts or turns head sideways when looking at TV, books, or objects
  • Frequent eye rubbing, squinting, or complaining of headaches
  • Abnormal white or mismatched reflection in pupil during flash photos

NetraScreen records experimental landmark observations. It cannot confirm or exclude an eye condition. Sudden vision loss, severe eye pain or a new curtain over vision require urgent medical care.
